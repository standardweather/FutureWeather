How these are made
  • IFR: model ceiling under 1,000 ft or visibility under 3 SM over at least half of a ~60 km box.
  • Mountain obscuration: cloud base at or below the ridge tops in mountainous terrain.
  • Turbulence: five diagnostics per layer (Ellrod TI1 and TI2, inverse Richardson number, frontogenesis, vertical shear), each mapped to an eddy dissipation rate scale fitted on past GFS runs and averaged, plus boundary-layer and mountain wave terms. Moderate is EDR 0.22 or more; split at FL180.
  • Surface wind and LLWS: sustained 10 m wind of 30 kt or more; 30 kt or more of wind change between 10 m and 2,000 ft AGL.
  • Icing: supercooled cloud and rain water, temperature, cloud-top temperature and lift, level by level. Freezing levels come from the raw temperature profile.
  • Convective (HRRR): simulated reflectivity of 40 dBZ or more covering 40% of an area, drawn as an area, a line or an isolated cell, with echo tops, motion between model hours, and supercell, hail and wind tags.

Areas under 3,000 sq mi are left out, as for real AIRMETs. Thresholds are first guesses and have not been checked against pilot reports yet.
